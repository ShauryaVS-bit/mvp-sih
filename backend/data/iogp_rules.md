# IOGP Life-Saving Rules — Reference Document

## Overview

The IOGP (International Association of Oil & Gas Producers) Life-Saving Rules represent the minimum mandatory safety behaviors that must be observed by all workers in the oil and gas industry. These rules are derived from analysis of fatal incidents across the global oil and gas industry. Violation of these rules is the leading cause of worker fatalities in the sector.

The IOGP Life-Saving Rules are based on the principle that certain tasks carry a disproportionate risk of fatality if not controlled correctly. A fatality does not require a complex sequence of events — in many cases, a single uncontrolled act is sufficient to cause death.

---

## Rule 1: Work with a valid Permit to Work when required

A Permit to Work (PTW) must be in place before starting any non-routine hazardous work. The PTW ensures that all hazards have been identified, control measures are in place, and authorization has been granted by a competent authority.

**Do This:**
- Obtain a valid Permit to Work before starting the job.
- Ensure all conditions and precautions in the PTW are met before starting work.
- Stop work if conditions change and re-assess.

**Never Do This:**
- Work without a Permit to Work when one is required.
- Proceed with work if the PTW has expired or conditions have changed significantly.
- Bypass or circumvent the PTW system.

**Relevance to Major Incidents:** Many major industrial accidents (Piper Alpha 1988, Texas City 2005) have been linked to failures in Permit to Work systems, including expired permits, miscommunication during shift handovers, and unauthorized bypass of PTW requirements.

## Rule 2: Conduct a gas test when required

Gas testing must be performed before and during any work where there is a risk of encountering flammable or toxic gas. This includes hot work, confined space entry, and work on or near process pipework and vessels.

**Do This:**
- Test the atmosphere before starting work.
- Conduct continuous monitoring during high-risk work (e.g., hot work adjacent to process equipment).
- Use calibrated, bump-tested gas detectors.
- Record gas test results on the Permit to Work.

**Never Do This:**
- Assume the atmosphere is safe without testing.
- Work without gas monitoring in areas where hydrocarbons or toxic gases may be present.
- Ignore or silence gas alarms without investigating the cause.

**Key Clause 2.3:** If a gas alarm activates during work, all work must stop immediately. Personnel must evacuate to the designated muster point. Work shall not resume until the source is identified, eliminated, and the area is declared gas-free by a competent person.

**Key Clause 2.5:** All gas detectors used for pre-work gas testing shall be calibrated against certified reference gas mixtures. Detectors with expired calibration certificates shall not be used.

## Rule 3: Verify isolation before work begins

Before performing any work on equipment containing, or that previously contained, energy sources (pressure, electrical, mechanical, thermal, chemical, potential), those energy sources must be isolated, locked out/tagged out (LOTO), and verified as zero energy before work commences.

**Do This:**
- Identify all energy sources associated with the equipment.
- Isolate each energy source using positive isolation methods (blanking, blinding, spade, double-block-and-bleed).
- Apply a personal lock and tag to each isolation point.
- Verify isolation by testing (pressure gauge reads zero, voltage tester reads zero, attempting to start equipment).

**Never Do This:**
- Rely solely on a closed valve as isolation for high-pressure or high-energy systems without secondary isolation (double block and bleed).
- Assume that equipment is isolated without personally verifying the isolation.
- Remove isolation (locks, tags, blinds) without authorization from the work authority.

**Key Clause 3.1 — Double Block and Bleed:** For work on high-pressure pipelines, wellheads, and manifolds involving potential hydrocarbon release, a single closed valve is insufficient isolation. A Double Block and Bleed (DBB) configuration is mandatory: two in-line block valves in series (both confirmed CLOSED) with a bleed valve between them (confirmed OPEN and venting to safe location). The bleed valve reading must confirm zero pressure before work.

**Key Clause 3.4 — Energy Verification:** Mechanical zero-pressure verification shall be performed by observing a calibrated pressure gauge at the work point, not at the isolation point. A gauge reading of zero at the isolation valve does not confirm zero pressure at the downstream work location.

**Key Clause 3.7 — Secondary Barrier Integrity:** In well intervention and manifold pressure operations, when primary isolation (Valve A) is opened for bleed-down, the secondary isolation valve (Valve B) must be positively confirmed CLOSED before commencement. Omission of this confirmation represents a critical barrier gap even when the primary operation appears to complete without incident.

**Historical Linkage:** The 2020 Baghjan-5 Well Blowout (Assam, India) involved operations during which secondary isolation integrity was compromised during a pressure bleed operation. The absence of verified secondary barrier status is cited as a contributing factor in the post-incident investigation.

## Rule 4: Obtain authorization before overriding or disabling safety critical equipment

Safety-critical equipment — including Emergency Shutdown Devices (ESD), Pressure Safety Valves (PSV), Fire and Gas Detection systems, and Safety Instrumented Systems (SIS) — must never be bypassed, overridden, or disabled without formal authorization through a Management of Change (MoC) process.

**Do This:**
- Obtain formal written authorization (MoC approval, Bypass Approval Form) before disabling any safety device.
- Implement compensatory measures that provide equivalent protection during the bypass period.
- Set a maximum duration for the bypass and restore the system before that deadline.
- Notify all affected personnel and the control room of the bypass status.

**Never Do This:**
- Override or defeat a safety system without written authorization.
- Leave a safety system bypassed beyond the approved duration.
- Disable multiple interdependent safety systems simultaneously.

**Key Clause 4.2:** The simultaneous bypass of two or more layers of protection in the same hazard pathway (e.g., bypassing both the high-pressure shutdown and the PSV simultaneously) constitutes a Critical Safety Barrier Degradation event and requires immediate escalation to the highest on-site authority.

## Rule 5: Do not walk past an unsafe act or condition

All personnel have the authority and the obligation to stop any work that they believe is unsafe. This "Stop Work Authority" (SWA) is unconditional and must be exercised without fear of reprisal.

**Do This:**
- Stop work and address any unsafe act or condition immediately.
- Report all near-misses, unsafe acts, and unsafe conditions to the supervisor.
- Participate in incident investigations constructively.

**Never Do This:**
- Walk past an unsafe act or condition without intervening.
- Accept rationalized explanations for bypassing safety controls.
- Discourage or retaliate against anyone who exercises Stop Work Authority.

## Rule 6: Follow the requirements for working in a confined space

Before entering any confined space (tanks, vessels, pits, sumps, trenches, pipe interiors), a confined space entry procedure must be followed. The atmosphere must be tested, a standby person must be posted at the entry point, rescue equipment must be available, and a valid confined space permit must be in place.

**Key Clause 6.1 — Atmospheric Testing:** The confined space atmosphere must be tested for oxygen level (minimum 19.5%, maximum 23.5%), flammable gas (maximum 10% of LEL), and toxic gases (H2S < 1 ppm, CO < 25 ppm) before entry. Testing shall be conducted at multiple heights within the space (top, middle, bottom) due to density stratification of different gases.

**Key Clause 6.3 — Standby Person:** A trained standby person must be posted at the entry point for the entire duration of confined space work. The standby person must not enter the space under any circumstances, even to attempt rescue. Rescue must be performed by trained rescue team members with proper equipment.

**Never Do This:**
- Enter a confined space without atmospheric testing.
- Remain in a confined space without a standby person present.
- Attempt rescue from a confined space without proper equipment and training.

## Rule 7: Do not place yourself under a suspended load

Under no circumstances should any person stand or work beneath a suspended load. All crane lifts must have an established exclusion zone, and the load path must be clear of personnel before and during the lift.

**Key Clause 7.2:** The exclusion zone radius for a crane lift must be at minimum equal to the lift height plus 20% as a safety margin. This zone must be barricaded and signage posted. A dedicated lift supervisor must visually confirm the exclusion zone is clear before lift commences and must maintain constant surveillance during the lift.

**Key Clause 7.5 — Slinging and Rigging:** All rigging equipment (slings, shackles, spreader bars) must have valid load testing certificates and must be inspected before use. Damaged or uncertified rigging equipment shall not be used.

**Never Do This:**
- Stand or work under a suspended load.
- Allow a lift to proceed without establishing an exclusion zone.
- Use damaged or uncertified rigging equipment.

---

## Rule 8: Do not operate equipment without proper authorization

All equipment — including vehicles, cranes, pressure testing equipment, and process equipment — must only be operated by personnel who are trained, authorized, and competent to operate it.

---

## Rule 9: Follow safe driving rules

All vehicle movement on site must comply with site traffic management rules. Speed limits must be observed, seatbelts must be worn, and mobile phones must not be used while driving.

---

## Rule 10: Do not use alcohol or drugs while working

The use of alcohol, illicit drugs, or prescription medications that impair performance is strictly prohibited while working or on standby for work.

---

## Rule 11: Follow the requirements for working at height

Any work conducted at height (>1.8 meters / 6 feet above a lower level) requires a fall prevention plan. Fall arrest equipment must be worn and anchored to a certified anchor point when working at height.

**Key Clause 11.1 — Fall Arrest Systems:** Personnel working at height must wear a full-body harness inspected before each use. The harness must be connected to a certified anchor point rated for minimum 22 kN (5,000 lbf) static load. Lanyards with shock absorbers must be used. Free-fall distance must be calculated to ensure the worker does not contact the lower level before arrest.

**Key Clause 11.4 — Inspection:** All fall protection equipment must have a valid annual inspection by a competent person. Equipment that has been subjected to fall forces must be immediately withdrawn from service and inspected before re-use.

**Never Do This:**
- Work at height without a fall arrest system unless protected by a physical barrier (guardrail) at all edges.
- Anchor a fall arrest lanyard to an uncertified or uninspected point.
- Use fall protection equipment that has been involved in a previous fall without inspection.

## Rule 12: Follow the requirements for working with H2S

In areas where hydrogen sulfide (H2S) may be present, gas detection must be active, breathing apparatus must be immediately available, and escape routes must be clear. Positive-pressure self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) must be worn when H2S concentrations may exceed 10 ppm.

**Key Clause 12.1:** The IDLH (Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health) concentration of H2S is 100 ppm. At this concentration, immediate escape is impossible without SCBA. The olfactory (smell) sense is rapidly fatigued by H2S at concentrations above 50 ppm, making odor an unreliable warning above this level. Gas detectors are the only reliable warning system.

**Key Clause 12.3:** All personnel working in H2S zones must receive H2S awareness training, including recognition of symptoms, alarm response, and SCBA donning. Annual refresher training is mandatory.

**Never Do This:**
- Enter an H2S zone without a functioning personal H2S monitor.
- Attempt to rescue a H2S victim without SCBA.
- Rely on smell as an indicator of H2S safety.
