# OISD Standards Reference — Process Safety Guidelines

## Overview

The Oil Industry Safety Directorate (OISD) is a technical directorate under the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, Government of India. OISD formulates and coordinates the implementation of a series of self-regulatory measures aimed at enhancing safety standards in the oil and gas industry in India. OISD standards are mandatory for oil and gas operations in India and represent best practices aligned with international standards such as API, ISO, and IEC.

## OISD-RP-238: Well Integrity Management

### Scope

This recommended practice applies to all exploration, appraisal, development, and production wells operated by OISD member companies. It covers well design, construction, completion, workover, and abandonment phases with specific emphasis on barrier management to prevent uncontrolled hydrocarbon release.

### Section 4: Barrier Philosophy

A well barrier is an envelope of one or several well barrier elements (WBE) preventing fluids from flowing unintentionally from the formation into the wellbore, or from the wellbore to the environment.

**Clause 4.1 — Two Barrier Principle:**
All production wells shall maintain at least two independent and tested well barriers at all times. A well must never be operated with only one barrier in place without formal risk assessment and compensatory measures approved by the Well Integrity Engineer.

**Clause 4.3 — Primary Barrier:**
The primary barrier is the innermost barrier preventing flow from the reservoir to the wellbore. For surface operations, this typically comprises the production packer, tubing, and downhole safety valve (DHSV).

**Clause 4.5 — Secondary Barrier:**
The secondary barrier provides backup protection in case of primary barrier failure. For wellhead and manifold operations, the secondary barrier typically includes the surface wellhead valves in a double block and bleed configuration.

**Clause 4.7 — Barrier Verification:**
Barriers must be tested and verified at specified intervals. A barrier cannot be relied upon if its last test date exceeds the maximum allowable test interval defined in the Well Integrity Management Plan (WIMP). An unverified barrier shall be treated as a FAILED barrier for operational planning purposes.

### Section 6: Manifold and Surface Operations

**Clause 6.1 — Pre-Operation Verification:**
Before any surface operation that involves opening or closing wellhead valves (including pressure bleed-down, chemical injection, well testing, and workover), the operator must complete a valve lineup verification sheet. This sheet must document the open/closed status of each valve in the manifold, signed by both the operator and the supervisor.

**Clause 6.2 — Double Block and Bleed (DBB) Configuration:**
For wellhead manifold pressure bleed-down operations, a Double Block and Bleed (DBB) configuration is mandatory. Two block valves (typically the master valve and wing valve, or two separate isolation valves) must be confirmed CLOSED in series, with a bleed valve between them confirmed OPEN to a safe flare or vent point. The pressure gauge downstream of the DBB must read ZERO before any downstream work commences.

**Clause 6.2.1 — Secondary Valve Verification in Bleed Operations:**
When the primary bleed valve (Valve A) is opened to depressurize a wellhead section or manifold spool, the secondary isolation valve (Valve B) must be independently confirmed CLOSED before and after the bleed operation. The confirmation must be documented. Verbal confirmation alone is insufficient; physical valve position verification is required.

**Clause 6.2.2 — Implicit Barrier Gap:**
If an incident report or operational report describes a pressure bleed operation but does not explicitly state the confirmation status of the secondary isolation valve, this represents an Implicit Barrier Gap. The secondary valve status is UNKNOWN, not CONFIRMED CLOSED. Operations shall not proceed or shall be suspended until the secondary valve status is physically verified and documented.

**Clause 6.4 — Pressure Monitoring During Bleed:**
During manifold or wellhead bleed-down, a calibrated pressure gauge must be monitored throughout the operation. If pressure does not drop to zero, or drops and then rises again (indicating upstream isolation leakage), work must stop and the situation re-assessed.

### Section 7: Well Intervention and Workover

**Clause 7.1 — Pre-Workover Safety Case:**
A Well Intervention Safety Case (WISC) must be prepared and approved before any workover or intervention that involves breaching a well barrier. The WISC must identify all potential hazard scenarios, required barriers, and emergency response procedures.

**Clause 7.5 — Blowout Prevention:**
All workover operations on wells with reservoir pressure > 34.5 bar (500 psi) must have Blowout Prevention Equipment (BOP) installed and tested before the primary well barrier is breached. The BOP test shall be documented in the Well Intervention Record.

## OISD-116: Fire Prevention and Protection System for Electronic Installations and Hotwork Control

### Section 3: Hotwork Permit and Gas-Free Certification

**Clause 3.1 — Hotwork Definition:**
Hotwork is defined as any work involving open flame, spark generation, or heat sufficient to ignite flammable materials. This includes welding, cutting, grinding, brazing, soldering, and the use of non-intrinsically safe electrical equipment in classified areas.

**Clause 3.3 — Gas-Free Certificate:**
Before any hotwork commences in or adjacent to classified areas (Zone 0, Zone 1, or Zone 2), a Gas-Free Certificate must be issued by a qualified safety officer or gas tester. The certificate must document:
- Results of gas testing (hydrocarbon concentration as % LEL)
- Time of test and validity period (maximum 2 hours; must be renewed before expiry)
- Ambient conditions (wind direction, weather)
- Any special conditions or restrictions

**Clause 3.5 — Fire Watch:**
A dedicated fire watch person must be present throughout any hotwork activity. The fire watch must remain at the hotwork location for at minimum 30 minutes after completion to detect smoldering. The fire watch must have immediate access to portable fire extinguisher rated for Class B fires.

**Clause 3.7 — Restricted Zones:**
Hotwork is prohibited within 15 meters of any open hydrocarbon process (open vents, process drains, open-top tanks, or active flowlines) without additional mitigating measures documented in the PTW.

## OISD-RP-175: Safety in Petroleum Refineries — Confined Space Entry

### Section 5: Confined Space Entry Procedure

**Clause 5.1 — Pre-Entry Testing:**
Atmospheric testing is mandatory before any person enters a confined space. Testing must be performed for:
- Oxygen content: 19.5%–23.5% (acceptable range)
- Combustible gases: < 10% LEL
- H2S: < 1 ppm (short-term exposure)
- CO (carbon monoxide): < 25 ppm

**Clause 5.2 — Standby Person Requirements:**
A trained standby person must be posted at the confined space entry point. The standby person must:
- Maintain continuous communication with personnel inside
- Track the number of persons inside the space
- Never enter the space, even in a rescue attempt
- Have immediate access to emergency communications

**Clause 5.4 — Rescue Equipment:**
Non-entry rescue equipment (tripod, winch, retrieval line attached to harness) must be set up and ready before any person enters a confined space. Entry rescue shall only be performed by trained rescue personnel with appropriate breathing apparatus.

## OISD-137: Electrical Safety in Petroleum Industry

### Section 8: Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

**Clause 8.1 — LOTO Requirement:**
Before any work on electrical equipment (maintenance, inspection, modification), the equipment must be de-energized and a LOTO procedure applied. LOTO involves physical disconnection of the power source, locking of the disconnect in the OFF position with a personal lock, and application of a danger tag.

**Clause 8.3 — Verification of Zero Energy:**
After applying LOTO, the worker must verify zero energy state by attempting to start the equipment (push start button while LOTO is applied) and by testing with an appropriate voltage tester at the work location. Successful LOTO isolation at the disconnecting means does not guarantee zero energy at the work point if there are multiple energy sources.

**Clause 8.5 — Electrical Arc Flash:**
All electrical work on energized equipment must be performed with appropriate arc flash PPE rated for the incident energy level at that location. Arc flash hazard analysis shall be performed for all electrical equipment where work may be performed without full de-energization.

## OISD-GDN-205: Safety in Lifting Operations

### Section 4: Lift Planning and Exclusion Zones

**Clause 4.1 — Lift Plan:**
All crane lifts must have a documented lift plan prepared by a competent rigger/lift supervisor before lifting commences. The lift plan must include: load weight (actual, not estimated), lift path, rigging arrangement, crane rated capacity at working radius, and exclusion zone dimensions.

**Clause 4.3 — Exclusion Zone:**
An exclusion zone must be established and barricaded around the lift area. The zone must extend to cover the entire area where the load could fall if the lift fails, including the area directly below the load at maximum swing. No personnel may enter the exclusion zone while a load is suspended.

**Clause 4.5 — Lift Supervisor:**
A dedicated lift supervisor must be present for all lifts. The lift supervisor must have clear communication with the crane operator at all times. If communication is lost, the lift must stop.

## OISD-GDN-206: Safety in Working at Height

### Section 3: Fall Protection Requirements

**Clause 3.1 — Fall Protection Mandatory:**
Full body harness and lanyard with energy absorber must be worn when working at height > 1.8 m (6 feet) above the lower level, where guardrails do not provide full perimeter protection.

**Clause 3.3 — Anchor Point Certification:**
Harness anchor points must be capable of withstanding 22 kN (5,000 lbf) static load. Anchor points must be inspected and certified by a competent person. Temporary anchor points (e.g., scaffold tubes, handrails) must be assessed for adequacy before use.

**Clause 3.5 — Scaffolding Inspection:**
All scaffolding must be inspected by a competent scaffolding inspector before use, after any modification, after any adverse weather event, and at minimum every 7 days during use. Uninspected scaffolding must be tagged "DO NOT USE" and barricaded.

## OISD-RP-187: Safety in Handling H2S

### Section 3: H2S Control Measures

**Clause 3.1 — Monitoring:**
In all operations where H2S may be present (sour well operations, sour crude handling, amine regeneration), personal H2S detectors must be worn by all personnel. Alarm set points: Low alarm at 5 ppm (move to fresh air), High alarm at 10 ppm (don SCBA immediately).

**Clause 3.4 — Entry into H2S Zones:**
Entry into any area designated as H2S Zone (where H2S concentration may exceed 10 ppm) requires:
- Positive-pressure SCBA (or supplied air breathing apparatus for extended work)
- Buddy system — minimum two persons
- Continuous communication with standby/safety observer
- H2S escape unit for emergency use

**Clause 3.6 — H2S Emergency Response:**
If a person is overcome by H2S, do NOT attempt rescue without SCBA. Activate the alarm, call for emergency response, and move to upwind muster point. Rescuers without SCBA must not enter the affected area.

## OISD-RP-147: Safety in Handling Hazardous Chemicals

### Section 5: Chemical Handling PPE

**Clause 5.1 — PPE Requirements:**
Before handling any hazardous chemical, the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) must be consulted to determine required PPE. Minimum requirements for corrosive chemicals (acids, caustics, oxidizers): chemical-resistant gloves, chemical splash goggles, face shield, chemical-resistant apron.

**Clause 5.3 — SDS Availability:**
Current SDS sheets for all chemicals in use must be available at the point of use. Personnel must be familiar with the SDS for all chemicals they handle, including emergency response procedures for spill and exposure.

## OISD-117: Static Electricity Hazards in Petroleum Industry

### Section 4: Bonding and Earthing During Fuel Transfer

**Clause 4.1 — Bonding Requirement:**
During transfer of flammable liquids (petroleum products, solvents, crude oil), the source container and receiving container must be bonded together with a metal conductor before transfer commences. This equalizes the electrostatic potential between the containers, preventing spark discharge.

**Clause 4.2 — Earthing Requirement:**
The combined bonded system must be connected to an earth ground before and during transfer. This dissipates accumulated static charge to earth. Transfer shall not commence until bonding and earthing connections are visually verified.

**Clause 4.4 — Tank Truck Earthing:**
Before the opening of any hatch or dome lid on a tanker truck carrying flammable petroleum products, the truck must be connected to an earth point using a dedicated earthing cable with alligator clip and continuity indicator. The continuity indicator must show a completed circuit before the hatch is opened.

## OISD-RP-189: Pressure Safety Valve Management

### Section 6: PSV Bypass Protocol

**Clause 6.1 — PSV Function:**
Pressure Safety Valves (PSVs) are the final line of defense against overpressure in process vessels, pipelines, and heat exchangers. They must never be bypassed without a formal Management of Change (MoC) process and compensatory measures.

**Clause 6.3 — MoC Requirement for Safety System Bypass:**
Any bypass of a PSV, ESD (Emergency Shutdown Device), or other safety-critical instrument must be authorized through the Management of Change process. The MoC must document: reason for bypass, duration of bypass, compensatory measures (e.g., manual pressure monitoring at reduced intervals), and approval by the site Safety Manager and Operations Manager.

**Clause 6.5 — Compensatory Measures:**
Compensatory measures during a PSV/ESD bypass must include enhanced manual monitoring at a frequency no less than every 15 minutes, immediate notification to the control room, and a pre-agreed escalation procedure if process parameters approach the set point of the bypassed device.

## OISD-105: Permit to Work System

### Section 3: PTW Requirements

**Clause 3.1 — PTW Applicability:**
A Permit to Work is required for all non-routine work including: maintenance of pressurized systems, electrical work (including instrumentation), hotwork in any location, confined space entry, excavation, lifting operations above 1 tonne, and any work that involves defeating a safety system.

**Clause 3.3 — PTW Validity:**
A PTW is valid only for the specific work, location, and time period stated on the permit. An expired PTW is invalid; work must stop, and a new PTW must be issued if work is to continue.

**Clause 3.5 — Site Conditions:**
Before initiating any work under a PTW, the issuing authority must physically verify that site conditions match those described in the permit and that all required isolations and control measures are in place.
